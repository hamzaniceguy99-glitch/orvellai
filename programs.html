The programs, in detail

Four programs of 6 to 12 weeks, at home or around your community studio time.

Program 01 · No wheel required

Hand-building

Hand-building is not the beginner version of pottery; it is a whole discipline. We work through pinch forms, coil construction, slab boxes and templates, and above all joins — scoring, slipping and drying evenly so nothing cracks as it dries.

  • 10 weeks
  • Complete beginners
  • 3 hours a week
  • Clay and a table

Start this program

Even walls, and getting a feel for the clay.

Weeks 4–6 · Coils

Building height without slumping.

Weeks 7–8 · Slabs

Templates, boxes and clean edges.

Weeks 9–10 · Drying

Even drying, and why pieces crack.

Weeks 1–4 · Centering

Bracing, water and patience.

Weeks 5–7 · Opening and pulling

Even walls, slow and deliberate.

Weeks 8–10 · Forms

Cylinders, bowls and repeats.

Weeks 11–12 · Trimming

Feet, weight and finishing.

Program 02 · Centering, then everything

Wheel throwing

Beginners film the pot; the answer is almost always in the hands and the posture. We work on wedging, bracing, consistent pressure and slow lifts, then opening, pulling and trimming — with weekly video review of thirty minutes at the wheel.

  • 12 weeks
  • Access to a wheel
  • 4 hours a week
  • Studio or home wheel

Start this program

Program 03 · Fewer unpleasant surprises

Glaze & firing

Glaze results depend on thickness, clay body and firing schedule, which is why the same bucket gives different results to different people. We work on consistent application, test tiles, reading crazing, pinholing and crawling, and matching glaze to cone. Food-safe means commercially certified and applied as instructed — nothing improvised.

  • 8 weeks
  • Firing somewhere
  • 3 hours a week
  • Test tiles

Start this program

Weeks 1–2 · Application

Thickness, dipping and brushing consistently.

Weeks 3–4 · Test tiles

A library of what your glazes actually do.

Weeks 5–6 · Faults

Crazing, pinholing, crawling and how to respond.

Weeks 7–8 · Food safety

Certified glazes, correct firing, honest limits.

Weeks 1–2 · Dust

Wet cleaning, storage and why dry sweeping is out.

Weeks 3–4 · Reclaim

Recycling clay without waste.

Weeks 5–6 · Your setup

Home or studio, planned realistically.

Program 04 · Safe, and sustainable

Studio practice

Clay dust is a real respiratory hazard: wet clean, never sweep dry, and don't let dry trimmings accumulate. We cover safe practice properly, how to reclaim clay, what a home setup genuinely requires — including that kiln installation is an electrician's job — and how to use a community studio well.

  • 6 weeks
  • Any level
  • 2 hours a week
  • A mop, not a broom
